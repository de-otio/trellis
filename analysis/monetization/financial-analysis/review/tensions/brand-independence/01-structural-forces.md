# Structural Forces

## The Core Dynamic

In the base case, brand value-exchange payments are 46% of total revenue — the single largest stream. If the model skews further toward value exchange (optimistic scenario), it could reach 50%+.

When nearly half your revenue comes from brand payments, brands have implicit leverage. They don't need to make explicit threats — the platform naturally gravitates toward keeping its biggest revenue source happy. This happens through:

- **Decision framing**: When a product decision could go either way, the option that keeps brands happy is easier to justify ("it's good for revenue")
- **Attention allocation**: Brand partner needs get faster responses, more product attention, more roadmap priority
- **Risk asymmetry**: Losing a brand has immediate, measurable revenue impact; losing user trust has diffuse, delayed impact
- **Normalization**: Small accommodations compound over time until the platform is effectively brand-serving

This is the same structural force that made ad-supported social platforms optimize for advertisers over users. The mechanism is identical; only the payment model differs.

---

## How Trellis's Model Is Structurally Better

The value-exchange model has four properties that make it more resistant to brand capture than traditional advertising:

### 1. Users Choose Brands (Brands Don't Buy Placement)

In ad-supported platforms, brands pay for access to users. Users have no say in which brands appear. In Trellis's model, users opt in to brands they genuinely like. This means:
- Brand leverage is limited to brands users already chose
- Unpopular brands can't buy their way to prominence
- User preference is the primary distribution mechanism

### 2. Revenue Is Per-Action (Not Per-Impression)

Ad models pay per impression, which incentivizes maximizing impressions (infinite scroll, autoplay, notification spam). Trellis's model pays per completed value action. This means:
- More time on platform ≠ more brand revenue (unless users complete more actions)
- The platform has no incentive to maximize screen time for brand revenue
- Value actions have a weekly cap, structurally limiting brand revenue extraction

### 3. Quality Standards Are Platform-Wide

Ad platforms have per-advertiser rules (premium placement for bigger spenders, custom content policies for enterprise accounts). Trellis's quality thresholds are platform-wide — every review, every Q&A answer, every recommendation is scored against the same bar.

### 4. Revenue Is Diversified

54% of revenue comes from non-brand sources:
- Premium subscriptions: 17%
- Ambient brand revenue: 15%
- B2B brand tools: 22%

Losing all brand value-exchange revenue (Stream 2) would be severe but not fatal — the other three streams cover costs at scale.

---

## Why the Structural Advantage Is Fragile

These four properties only hold if they're actively maintained. Each one can be eroded:

| Property | How It Could Erode |
|---|---|
| Users choose brands | "Suggested brands" feature algorithmically promotes high-paying brands |
| Per-action revenue | Adding "brand impressions" as a secondary metric that brands can also pay for |
| Platform-wide quality | "Premium brand tier" with lower quality thresholds for enterprise partners |
| Revenue diversification | Deprioritizing subscription/B2B investment because brand revenue is growing faster |

None of these erosions would happen as a single decision. They'd accumulate through dozens of small, individually reasonable product choices over years. This is why structural safeguards matter more than good intentions.

---

## The Year 2 Vulnerability Window

Brand dependency risk is not uniform over time:

| Period | Brand Count | Top Brand Leverage | Overall Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 5-10 | High (30-40% of brand revenue) but brand revenue is ~0% of total | **Low** — social-first phase, no brand dependency |
| Year 2 | 35 | Medium (15-20%) but brand revenue is 46% of total | **High** — few brands, high revenue share |
| Year 3 | 75 | Lower (10-12%) and other streams growing | **Medium** — diversifying but still concentrated |
| Year 5 | 300 | Low (3-5%) | **Low** — natural diversification |

**Year 2 is the critical period.** The platform has enough brand revenue to depend on it, but too few brands to survive the loss of any major partner. Safeguards must be in place before Year 2, not after.
