# Brand Partnership Principles

Draft principles for the brand partnership agreement. These codify Trellis's editorial independence as contractual commitments, not just internal policy.

---

## Purpose

These principles serve two functions:
1. **External**: Set clear expectations with brand partners from day one. Brands that agree to these terms are self-selecting for alignment with Trellis's values.
2. **Internal**: Create a contractual obligation that prevents the platform from accommodating brand pressure, even under revenue pressure. The principles are a constraint on the platform, not just on brands.

---

## Draft Principles

### Principle 1: Platform Editorial Independence

The platform retains sole discretion over:
- Quality standards and scoring algorithms
- Content policies and moderation rules
- Value action types and their design
- Feed algorithms and content ranking
- Transparency and disclosure practices
- User experience design

Brand partners acknowledge that these decisions are made in the interest of platform users, not brand partners. The platform will not modify these policies at the request or to the benefit of any individual brand.

### Principle 2: Authentic Content Only

Value actions produce authentic user content. The platform will never:
- Require or incentivize positive content
- Suppress negative but honest content
- Filter content based on brand preference
- Provide brands with tools to influence content sentiment

Quality thresholds are applied uniformly across all brands and content types. Content is filtered for quality (effort, helpfulness, relevance), never for sentiment.

### Principle 3: User Choice and Agency

Users choose which brands to engage with and which action types to perform. The platform will never:
- Assign users to brands
- Algorithmically promote specific brands to users
- Allow brands to target specific users
- Steer users toward specific action types on behalf of brands

Brand budget is allocated by user preference. If users prefer reviews over recommendations, the brand pays for reviews. The brand cannot redirect budget toward preferred action types.

### Principle 4: Architectural Separation

Brand-related content exists in the Contribution Space, architecturally separate from the social feed. The platform will never:
- Insert brand-related content into the social feed algorithmically
- Create action types whose completion criteria involves social feed posting
- Use social feed data to optimize brand content distribution
- Allow brand analytics to access social feed engagement data

Users may voluntarily share their contributions to their social feed, with full transparency labeling. This is user-initiated, never platform-initiated or brand-requested.

### Principle 5: Data Minimization

Brand partners receive aggregated, anonymized analytics only. The platform will never:
- Share individual user data with brands
- Identify specific users to brands (even pseudonymously)
- Provide brands with user contact information
- Share user data across brands

Users with `analyticsOptOut` enabled are excluded from all brand analytics entirely.

### Principle 6: Equal Treatment

All brand partners are subject to identical terms, thresholds, and policies. The platform will never:
- Create "premium" or "enterprise" brand tiers with different quality standards
- Offer preferential quality scoring to higher-paying brands
- Provide different content policies based on brand spend
- Allow any brand to influence policies that apply to other brands

Pricing tiers (Starter, Professional, Enterprise) differ in tool access and support level, never in editorial treatment.

### Principle 7: Transparency

The platform publishes a quarterly transparency report that includes:
- Number of active brand partners
- Aggregate brand revenue range
- Category diversity of brand partners
- Number of brand requests declined, with anonymized reasons
- Quality threshold calibration data
- User sentiment toward brand content

Brand partners are informed of the transparency report as part of the partnership agreement.

## Implementation Notes

### When to Formalize

These principles should be formalized into a legal document before the first brand partnership agreement is signed (Phase 2, Month 4-8 of go-to-market). A lawyer should review them for enforceability in DACH jurisdictions.

### How to Present to Brands

Frame as a strength, not a constraint: "We maintain editorial independence because that's what makes our user content authentic — and authentic content is why your investment in our platform generates higher ROI than traditional advertising. These principles protect the value of your partnership."

Brands that object to these principles are brands that want to manipulate content — exactly the partners Trellis should avoid.

### How to Enforce Internally

- Post the principles visibly (internal wiki, onboarding docs)
- Reference specific principles when declining brand requests
- Include principle compliance in the quarterly transparency report
- If a principle is ever violated, document the violation, correct it, and disclose it in the transparency report

### Evolution

These principles may evolve as the platform grows. Changes should be:
- Documented with rationale
- Never made at the request of a brand partner
- Never made under revenue pressure
- Reviewed by an independent party (advisor, board member, or external reviewer)
- Applied equally to all existing and future brand partners
