# Gamification

Gamification is necessary to encourage participation but must be carefully designed to avoid recreating the addictive mechanics that this platform exists to oppose.

---

## Principles

### Do

- Use points to unlock cosmetic features or access tiers
- Frame progress as *"you've earned enough to unlock the app for this month"*
- Reward quality over quantity
- Celebrate milestones without creating FOMO for missing them
- Allow users to turn off all gamification elements

### Don't

- Use streaks that punish absence
- Create leaderboards that trigger social anxiety or compulsive comparison
- Implement dopamine-loop mechanics (variable reward schedules, notification spam)
- Gate essential features behind gamification progress
- Create artificial scarcity or urgency ("only 2 hours left to earn your badge!")

---

## Proposed Mechanics

### Access Credits

- Users earn credits for each completed value action
- Credits convert directly to platform access time (e.g., 1 month)
- Clear, predictable conversion rate — no randomness
- Surplus credits roll over (no "use it or lose it")

### Quality Badges

- Earned for consistently high-quality contributions (e.g., reviews rated helpful by peers)
- Cosmetic only — visible on profile, no functional advantage
- Awarded infrequently to maintain meaning
- No penalty for not earning them

### Contribution Tiers

- Bronze / Silver / Gold based on cumulative contribution quality
- Tiers unlock cosmetic profile features (themes, frames)
- Tiers do not affect social features or visibility
- No decay — once earned, a tier is kept

---

## Anti-Addiction Guardrails

- **No variable rewards** — every action has a known, fixed credit value
- **No notification pressure** — notifications are opt-in and batched (e.g., weekly summary)
- **No social comparison by default** — leaderboards, if they exist, are opt-in and private
- **Usage time tracking** — show users how long they've spent on the platform with gentle nudges
- **Cool-down periods** — after completing value actions, encourage users to leave the app

## Open Questions

- How do we measure whether our gamification is staying non-addictive over time?
- Should we publish our gamification design principles as a public commitment?
- What's the minimum gamification needed to sustain engagement, and can we stay at that minimum?
- How do we handle the tension between "making the app engaging" and "not being addictive"?
