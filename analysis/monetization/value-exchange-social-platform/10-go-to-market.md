# Go-to-Market Strategy

The value-exchange model has a cold-start problem: brands need users, users need a social product, and the value-exchange layer needs both. This document outlines how to bootstrap the model.

---

## Phased Launch

### Phase 1: Social-First (Months 1-6)

Build the dog-fan community without any value-exchange mechanics.

- Launch Trellis as a social platform for dog fans
- Focus on community features: posts, photos, groups, messaging
- Monetize with a simple subscription ($3-5/month) or free tier with limits
- Goal: establish a genuine, engaged community of 5,000-10,000 active users

**Why social-first**: The social product must stand on its own. If the community only exists because of the value exchange, it's not a real community — it's a task marketplace.

### Phase 2: Brand Partnerships (Months 4-8, overlapping)

Recruit founding brand partners while the community grows.

- Target 5-10 dog-related brands (pet food, accessories, veterinary services)
- Offer favorable terms: reduced pricing, exclusivity in their category, early data access
- Co-design the first value actions with these brands
- Validate that brands see real value in authentic user contributions

**Brand selection criteria**:
- Genuine relevance to the dog-fan community
- Ethical reputation (no puppy mills, no controversial practices)
- Willingness to accept honest feedback (including negative)
- Financial stability to commit to a 6-12 month pilot

### Phase 3: Value-Exchange Launch (Months 6-12)

Introduce the value-exchange layer to existing users.

- Announce the value-exchange option: "Continue using Trellis for free by contributing to brands you like"
- Gradual rollout: start with a small percentage of users, expand based on feedback
- Keep the subscription option prominently available
- Monitor all anti-addiction and transparency metrics from day one

### Phase 4: Scale (Months 12+)

Expand brand partnerships and user base.

- Open self-serve brand onboarding portal
- Expand to adjacent verticals (pet services, outdoor gear, family activities)
- International expansion (DACH first, then broader EU, then US)
- Publish first transparency and anti-addiction reports

## Early Adoption Mechanics

### For Users

- **Founding member status**: Early users get permanent recognition (badge, tier)
- **Grandfathered pricing**: Early subscribers keep their rate if they switch to value-exchange later
- **Community ownership feel**: Early users help shape brand selection and value action design
- **No bait-and-switch**: Users who joined for the social product should never feel forced into value actions

### For Brands

- **Founding partner program**: First 10 brands get exclusivity, reduced rates, and direct product input
- **Case study commitment**: Brands agree to let the platform publish anonymized results
- **Co-creation**: Brands help design action types that are genuinely useful (not just marketing theater)
- **Transparent metrics**: Brands see exactly what they're getting — no inflated numbers

## Geographic Strategy

### Start: DACH Region (Germany, Austria, Switzerland)

- Strong existing Trellis user base and brand awareness
- High willingness to pay for quality products and ethical practices
- Strict regulatory environment forces good habits early (GDPR, consumer protection)
- Dog culture is significant (8M+ dogs in Germany alone)

### Expand: Broader EU

- Similar regulatory framework (GDPR consistent)
- Cultural affinity for the "transparent, non-exploitative" positioning
- Established pet industry with known brands

### Later: US / Global

- Largest digital advertising market
- FTC endorsement rules are well-defined
- Higher competition but larger total addressable market

## Key Metrics by Phase

| Phase | Primary Metric | Target |

| Phase 1 | Monthly active users | 5,000-10,000 |
| Phase 1 | User retention (30-day) | > 40% |
| Phase 2 | Brand partners signed | 5-10 |
| Phase 2 | Brand satisfaction score | > 4/5 |
| Phase 3 | Value-exchange adoption rate | 30-50% of active users |
| Phase 3 | User sentiment (value exchange) | > 70% positive |
| Phase 4 | Revenue per user | > $4/month |
| Phase 4 | Brand retention (annual) | > 80% |

## Risks Specific to Go-to-Market

- **Premature value-exchange launch**: Introducing brand actions before the community is strong enough poisons the well. Wait until the social product is genuinely loved.
- **Wrong founding brands**: If early brands are seen as spammy or irrelevant, users will reject the entire concept.
- **Underfunding Phase 1**: The social-first phase generates minimal revenue. Need enough runway to sustain 6-12 months before brand payments begin.
- **Competitor copying**: The model is easy to describe but hard to execute. Speed matters less than execution quality.
