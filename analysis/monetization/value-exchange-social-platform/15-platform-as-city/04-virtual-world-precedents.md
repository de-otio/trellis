# Adjacent — Virtual-World Political Economy

Pre-existing virtual polities that have been studied academically as polities and economies. Not on the critical path for the hypothesis — none of them used a tax model in the sense the hypothesis requires — but they establish that **inside-platform governance and inside-platform economics can be treated as real**, which is the precondition for the city analogy.

---

## Core Works

| Author / Work | Year | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Edward Castronova, *Synthetic Worlds: The Business and Culture of Online Games*** (Chicago) | 2005 | The founding treatment of virtual worlds as real economies. Castronova famously calculated that EverQuest's "Norrath" had a higher per-capita GDP than several real countries. Establishes the move from "the platform is metaphorically like a polity" to "the platform IS a polity, formally treated." |
| **Tom Boellstorff, *Coming of Age in Second Life: An Anthropologist Explores the Virtually Human*** (Princeton) | 2008 | Ethnographic treatment of Second Life as a society. Useful for the *citizenship* part of the hypothesis — what does it mean to be a citizen of an in-platform polity? |
| **EVE Online's Council of Stellar Management (CSM)** | 2008– | A player-elected representative body that meets with the developer (CCP Games). Operational example of formal in-platform political representation. Multiple academic treatments. |
| **vTaiwan / Polis (Audrey Tang)** | 2015– | Not in-game, but a deliberation platform that has been treated formally as part of a polity. The connecting tissue between virtual-world political economy and the Plurality programme in [02-mechanism-design-and-funding.md](02-mechanism-design-and-funding.md). |

---

## What This Lineage Gives the Hypothesis

Establishes that **in-platform polities are not metaphorical**. Once a platform reaches a certain scale and stakes, it acquires polity-like properties whether or not its operators frame it that way. This is the strongest available argument against the response *"the platform-as-city framing is just an analogy."*

Castronova in particular gives the hypothesis its *economic* footing: in-platform economies are real, measurable, and have already been studied as such.

---

## What This Lineage Does Not Give

A **funding model**. EVE's CSM is governance, not finance. Second Life's economy was speculative and rent-based, not tax-based. None of the virtual-world precedents financed their common services from internal taxation in a way the hypothesis can directly inherit.

It also doesn't bridge to the **B2B/B2C distinction**. Most virtual-world citizen models treat all in-world actors as broadly similar; the B2B/B2C divide is specific to social-platforms-with-commerce, not virtual worlds.
