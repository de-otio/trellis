# Reading List — Prioritised

Prioritised reading order against the hypothesis (platform-as-city, B2B/B2C as co-citizens, tax-financed common services). The full lineage tables are in [01](01-platforms-as-public-infrastructure.md), [02](02-mechanism-design-and-funding.md), [03](03-platform-cooperativism.md), [04](04-virtual-world-precedents.md); this document is for choosing a finite reading slice.

---

## If You Read One Thing

**Glen Weyl & Audrey Tang, *Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy* (2024).** The closest existing synthesis of platform-as-polity with tax-shaped funding mechanisms. Available in book form and as a freely available online text.

---

## If You Read Three Things

In addition to *Plurality*:

1. **Buterin, Hitzig & Weyl, "A Flexible Design for Funding Public Goods"** (Management Science 2019; preprint earlier). The QF paper. The mechanism is the most concretely-relevant single contribution to claim 3.
2. **K. Sabeel Rahman, "The New Utilities: Private Power, Social Infrastructure, and the Revival of the Public Utility Concept"** (Cardozo Law Review 2018). The strongest existing legal/political-theory case for claim 1.

---

## If You Read More — Reading-Order Guide

Order matters here. Read in this sequence rather than alphabetically:

1. The three above.
2. **Posner & Weyl, *Radical Markets* (2018)** — the COST and QV mechanisms in context. Fills in the framework around the QF paper.
3. **Ethan Zuckerman's writings on Digital Public Infrastructure (2022–)** — the operational vocabulary for the "common services" piece. Institute for Digital Public Infrastructure at UMass Amherst.
4. **Trebor Scholz, *Platform Cooperativism* (RLS NYC pamphlet, 2016)** — the most direct articulation of the non-tax alternative (ownership-based). Read to know what you are *not* proposing.
5. **Eli Pariser & Talia Stroud, "Civic Signals" / New_Public materials (2020–)** — design language for healthy public spaces. Operationally useful, not academically heavy.
6. **Mike Masnick, "Protocols, Not Platforms" (Knight Institute 2019)** — the federation-as-governance argument. Worth pairing with the existing [`../../../generic-core/11-activitypub-assessment.md`](../../../generic-core/11-activitypub-assessment.md).
7. **Benjamin Bratton, *The Stack: On Software and Sovereignty* (2015)** — the originating *Platform Polity* framing. Long, dense, and partially superseded by later work, but the headwaters.
8. **Edward Castronova, *Synthetic Worlds* (2005)** — for the case that in-platform polities are economically real, not metaphorical. Skim if time-constrained.

---

## What to Skip Unless Specifically Needed

- **Julie Cohen, *Between Truth and Power* (2019)** — important but adjacent. Read if the legal-construction-of-informational-capitalism framing is needed for a specific argument; otherwise Rahman + Bratton cover the territory.
- **Tom Boellstorff, *Coming of Age in Second Life* (2008)** — ethnography. Beautiful book, not on the critical path for this hypothesis.
- **Lars Doucet, *Land is a Big Deal* (2022)** — interesting for Georgist priors, but tangential unless the tax-base question becomes specifically "what is the natural tax base of digital activity?"
- **EVE Online CSM academic treatments** — interesting precedent for player-elected governance; not load-bearing for the funding model.
