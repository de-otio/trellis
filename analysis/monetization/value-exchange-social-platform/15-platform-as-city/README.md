# Platform-as-City — Research Survey

## Why This Folder Exists

A working hypothesis worth interrogating: **the platform is structurally like a city.** It provides common services that all users — B2B and B2C — depend on. Both classes are "citizens" of the platform in a meaningful sense, and the common services are financed by something tax-like rather than by ad extraction or ownership-asymmetry rent.

The hypothesis has three load-bearing claims:

1. The city analogy holds — platforms supply common services with public-good characteristics, not merely a marketplace.
2. B2B participants and B2C participants are **co-citizens** of the same polity, not buyer-and-seller across a market.
3. Common services are **tax-financed** by the participants who depend on them, not cross-subsidised by ad extraction or VC.

This folder surveys the research that supports each claim, names the lineages that have argued adjacent positions, and identifies the part of the synthesis that the literature does not currently supply. The Trellis-side implications are in [06-trellis-mapping.md](06-trellis-mapping.md).

## Bottom Line

The platform-as-city framing is well-supported in fragments and never assembled whole in the literature. The fragments are:

- *City analogy* — Bratton, Rahman, Zuckerman, Pariser.
- *Tax mechanism* — Buterin/Hitzig/Weyl, Posner & Weyl, Weyl & Tang.
- *Co-ownership* — Scholz and platform cooperativism.

The **dual B2B/B2C citizenship** claim is the most distinctive and the least supported by existing literature. Almost every adjacent treatment frames B2B participants as suppliers/customers/advertisers, not as fellow residents. That is the gap Trellis would write into. See [05-the-synthesis-gap.md](05-the-synthesis-gap.md).
