# Research Summary: Social Media Design and Adolescent Mental Health

## Sources

1. **NPR** (March 27, 2026): "Research points to how companies could make social media less addictive for teens" by Rhitu Chatterjee and Carmel Wroth
2. **AJPM** (April 2026): "Prospective Associations Between Early Adolescent Problematic Screen Use, Mental Health, Sleep, and Substance Use" — Nagata et al., *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*, 70(4):108248

## Legal Context

Two court verdicts in March 2026 underscore regulatory risk for social platforms:

- A **California jury** held Google and Meta responsible for depression and anxiety, concluding Instagram and YouTube were "deliberately built to be addictive"
- A **New Mexico jury** found Meta violates state laws and harms children's safety and mental health

## ABCD Study Key Findings (Nagata et al.)

**Study design:** Prospective cohort from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development Study — N=8,119 children aged 11-12 (47.5% female, 43.8% racial/ethnic minority), analyzed 2018-2020 with 1-year follow-up.

**Problematic social media use was prospectively associated with:**

| Outcome | Adjusted Odds Ratio / Beta |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Depressive problems | beta = 0.42 |
| Somatic problems | beta = 0.28 |
| Attention/deficit | beta = 0.23 |
| Oppositional defiant | beta = 0.27 |
| Conduct problems | beta = 0.29 |
| Weekly sleep duration | beta = -0.14 (shorter) |
| Suicidal behaviors | AOR = 1.86 |
| Sleep disturbance | AOR = 1.11 |
| Alcohol initiation | AOR = 1.22 |
| Tobacco initiation | AOR = 1.30 |
| Marijuana initiation | AOR = 1.66 |

**Critical finding on age verification:** Two-thirds of 11- and 12-year-olds already have social media accounts, with most having 3+ accounts. Current age verification (minimum age 13) is not effective.

**Addiction-like behaviors in 11-12 year olds:**
- 16% tried but failed to use social media less
- 23% spent a lot of time thinking about social media apps

## Expert Recommendations (from NPR)

**Dr. Jason Nagata** (UCSF) and **Dr. Mitch Prinstein** (UNC Chapel Hill) recommend:

1. Restrict or eliminate like/reaction notifications for minors
2. Limit notifications, especially at bedtime and during school hours
3. Restrict infinite scroll for minors
4. Restrict personalized/algorithmic feeds for minors
5. Default privacy settings to maximum protection for minors (no data sharing, no personalization)
6. Implement effective age verification
7. Provide meaningful parental controls

**Prinstein on the like feature:** "These are young folks who have a hypersensitive, social brain and a very weak prefrontal cortex... It's just too hard for adolescent brains to resist."
