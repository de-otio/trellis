# Competitive Differentiation: Safety as a Feature

How Trellis can use responsible design as a market advantage rather than just a compliance obligation.

## The Opportunity

The court verdicts against Meta and Google (March 2026) and the growing body of research create a market opening: parents and users increasingly want platforms that are designed responsibly. Most incumbents are retrofitting safety features onto engagement-maximized architectures. Trellis can build it right from the start.

The UN OHCHR guidance of 29 May 2026 sharpens this: it warns that age-based **bans** (Australia, Indonesia, Malaysia, with a dozen more countries weighing them) are easily circumvented and push children toward riskier, less-monitored spaces — "limiting access to platforms that remain unsafe cannot stand as the endpoint." A genuinely safe-by-design platform is precisely the thing regulators say bans *fail* to deliver. The posture is not "comply with the ban" but "be the platform that does not need one." See [07-safe-by-design-and-governance.md](07-safe-by-design-and-governance.md).

## Trellis's Existing Advantages

### 1. Sentiments > Likes

No other major platform uses an emotion vocabulary instead of binary likes. This is a defensible, research-aligned differentiator. Marketing angle: "We don't count your popularity — we help you understand how people feel."

### 2. Chronological Feed

While Instagram and TikTok optimize for engagement (time-on-app), Trellis shows posts in order. This is an active choice that prioritizes user agency over platform metrics.

### 3. Niche, Interest-Based Communities

A vertical built on Trellis serves a focused interest community (a hobby, a pet type, a local organization) rather than a general-purpose audience. An interest-bounded community is inherently lower-risk for the most harmful content categories identified by Prinstein: sexualized content, self-harm promotion, eating disorder content, and predatory behavior. The interest-based niche also means content personalization can be interest-driven (topics, activities) rather than engagement-driven.

### 4. ActivityPub Federation

Decentralized architecture means users aren't locked in. This is philosophically aligned with giving users control — a platform that lets you leave is one that has to earn your attention rather than trap it.

## Positioning Themes

### "Designed for Wellbeing"

Frame every safety feature as a positive user benefit, not a restriction:
- Paginated feeds → "We respect your time"
- No notification spam → "We won't interrupt your life"
- Sentiment vocabulary → "Real emotional connection, not popularity contests"
- Quiet hours → "Your sleep matters more than our engagement metrics"

### "Family-Friendly by Design"

Existing family-friendly compliance work (for example, a vertical's family-friendly feed-compatibility documentation) can be extended with the research-backed safety features:
- Age-appropriate experiences
- Parental controls that actually work
- Privacy defaults that protect by default

### Transparency

Publish a "Design Principles" page that explains _why_ Trellis makes these choices, citing the research. This builds trust with parents and aligns with the growing "humane technology" movement.

## What This Means for the Generic Core Goal

Trellis is a reusable social-network core with domain-specific extensions. Building responsible design patterns into the core — configurable age tiers, sentiment systems, feed friction, notification batching — makes the generic core more valuable. Any vertical (pet communities, hobby groups, local organizations) benefits from safety-first defaults.

These aren't features to bolt on later. They should be part of the core architecture.
